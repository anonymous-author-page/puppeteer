Puppeteer

Controlling Video World Models with Rigid 3D Puppets

TL;DR: Puppeteer uses complete object meshes and rigid trajectories in a shared 3D space to jointly control the camera, existing objects, and inserted objects in video world models.

Abstract

Directing a video world model requires control over the camera and every object. Cues in the image plane cannot express that control precisely in 3D. Proxies lifted into 3D from the input, such as tracked points or Gaussian blobs, capture only the visible shell of each object: they provide no geometry for unobserved regions, and moving them requires per-point trajectories that a user cannot specify. We introduce Puppeteer, a video world model driven by the Scene Puppet, a novel 4D representation with complete, fixed geometry and rigid motion. Each object is a canonical mesh, reconstructed once and moved only by a rigid transform per frame. Explicit 3D space removes that ambiguity and gives the user a more precise and intuitive interface. Complete object geometry avoids hallucinating an object's unobserved side under large motion or heavy occlusion. We render the puppet to a depth video and design a Puppet Motion Adapter that turns it into a video with high-fidelity, view-consistent appearance, illumination, and non-rigid motion. We further introduce Identity-Gated IoU (IG-IoU), which measures how accurately the generated object follows the specified motion while remaining the same object. Extensive experiments show that Puppeteer achieves higher visual quality and more accurate camera and object control than prior methods.
